flavorProfile: Mediterranean oregano belongs to the mint family and is robust,
  earthy, and slightly peppery, with a hint of bitterness and, as expected, a
  warm, minty aroma. Mexican oregano belongs to the verbenas plant family and
  has stronger citrus and anise notes.
culinaryUses:
  - Mediterranean oregano is typically used in dishes like pastas, pizza,
    vinaigrettes, and herbal teas.
  - "Mexican oregano is typically stronger in flavor so it's fantastic in
    chilis, beans, marinades, "
traditionalUses: Oregano is antibacterial, anti-fungal, and
  antibacterial.  Adding oregano to your food--whether into dishes or consuming
  as an herbal tea--could provide an added boost to your immune system to help
  heal your body faster and provide a little protection from minor seasonal
  illness.
growingNotes: Oregano is native to Europe and naturalized in the Middle East.
  It's a perennial in growing zones 4-9, and insanely easy to have available
  right outside your door.  Grows great in fertile, well draining soil, and
  would do best in a container to avoid unwanted spreading.
storage: Fresh oregano retains more valuable nutrients and oils that dried loses
  over time.  If you have it available, fresh is always better, but dried is
  perfectly fine as well.  Store fresh oregano stems in the refrigerator by
  wrapping the cut end in a wet paper towel and place the wrapped bundle inside
  a plastic bag left open. Fresh could last about 7-14 days this way.  Store dry
  oregano in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place.
